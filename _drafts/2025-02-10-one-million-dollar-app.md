In 2021 I locked myself in a room and began to code an app. It's now worth a million dollars.

By way of celebration I have submitted to this hard-hitting interview. Because I'm bootstrapped and was alone for the first few years, I am interviewing myself.

Let's get into it!

## Are you a millionaire now?

No. My business account fluctuates between 1.000 and 20.000 EUR. Currently around 20.000 EUR lands in this account every month. I pay myself a salary, but most of the cash immediately gets reinvested. This means paying my freelancers, of which there are 5. 

## How do you work out the value of your product?

It's a very rough back-of-the-envelope calculation.

I took my monthly revenue, multiplied it by 12 to get 240 000 EUR per year. Then I take a multiple of that.

SaaS products are valued by taking a multiple of their annual revenue, it can be as low as 2 or up to 7.

I am taking 4 to get to 1 million dollars. There is a lot of hand-waving involved in this step, I admit. But I am going to go with it. I wanted to write a blog post with a nice title, ok?

## How hard was it to do this?

It was both very hard and very easy. Let me explain.

What was hard was being alone and grinding away for months and months and eventually years. I think this may be difficult to understand if you haven't been through it yourself. 

On the one hand, there is positive feedback from the market. In my case, my revenue growth has been a straight line going up and to the right. This is awesome. But you don't see this during your daily grind. It just feels like you are doing the same thing again and again and often it feels like it's having zero effect.

Having feedback from the market is both a blessing and a curse. It's a blessing because you get a raw and unfiltered signal on whether your efforts are working. The curse is that often your efforts have zero effect on your bottom line. It can have terrible effect on your motivation when the dopamine hit of seeing your revenue increase doesn't materialize. To repeat myself: this hits hard, even when your overall situation is improving in the long term.
